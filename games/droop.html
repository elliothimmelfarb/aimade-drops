DROOP
National Control · Grid Frequency Desk · 1990
SHIFT HANDOVER NOTE — READ BEFORE YOU SIT DOWN
The whole country runs on one alternating current and it must turn at 50.00 cycles a second. Put in more power than the nation is drawing and the machines speed up; put in less and they slow down. The big dial in front of you is that speed. Hold it. 49.5–50.5 Hz is the law; ±0.2 Hz is your job.

You cannot see demand coming — but the TV listings on the wall can. When a programme ends, a million kettles go on inside two minutes. You know when. You never know exactly how big.

Coal is slow: order it now, it arrives twenty minutes from now. Gas is quick and dear. The pumped-storage set at Dinas Brân is your hammer — full output in seconds, but the upper reservoir empties. Governors on every set will help you automatically as frequency sags (that’s droop) — but only into the headroom you left them.

Below 49.5 the protection starts throwing regions off supply. Three blocks shed and you are relieved of duty.
