COINCIDENCE

NORTH SEA · 1916 · DIRECTOR TOP, H.M.S. INFLEXIBLE

Grey water to the horizon and a smudge of coal smoke twelve thousand yards off. You are the gunnery officer. Your four turrets throw a salvo of 12-inch shells that will be in the air for twenty seconds — you fire at where the enemy is going to be, and then you wait.

There is no hit marker. There are four white columns of water, and you read them. Short and they stand in front of the silhouette. Over and the hull cuts across their feet. Straddle — some short, some over — and that is where the hits live. Correct the range, correct the deflection, fire again. Walk them on.

Their fire-control is doing the same to you, and getting better every salvo. The old defence is to chase the splashes: steer toward the last cluster, because their next correction moves away from it. But a hard turn spoils your own solution as surely as theirs. Three ships come out of the mist in turn. Sink them all.

READING THE FALL OF SHOT

SHORT — the columns stand clear in front of the hull, low in the frame and large. Add range. OVER — the hull cuts across their feet, they are small and high. Take range off. STRADDLE — columns on both sides of her. Hold what you have and keep firing; that is where hits come from. The rangefinder shows a split image of the target: when the two halves line up, your set range is near right — but it lies more the further out and the thicker the mist. The splashes are the truth.
