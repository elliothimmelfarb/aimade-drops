JETSAM

Dover to Calais · 7 January 1785

Jean-Pierre Blanchard and Dr John Jeffries lift from the cliffs of Dover in a hydrogen balloon, carrying the first letters ever to cross a frontier by air. Halfway over, a bank of cloud takes the sun off the silk, the gas cools and contracts, and the Channel comes up to meet them. There is no burner. There is no engine that matters. There is only weight — and everything aboard, down to Blanchard's trousers, can be made to leave.

How she flies

A gas balloon has no throttle. Lift falls all afternoon as the sun weakens and hydrogen creeps through the silk, so she sinks unless you lighten her. One sandbag buys about 1.9 m/s of climb; two seconds on the valve costs about 1.5 m/s. Gas thrown away never returns — ballast is the cheap currency and the valve is the dear one. The statoscope needle at bottom left is your rate of climb; watch it before you watch the altitude.
