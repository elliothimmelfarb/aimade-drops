A rock the tide takes back

Skerry

Two pods of grey seals haul out on a low-water skerry. The tide is coming in on a schedule both of you can read, drowning the rock ledge by ledge. Take turns waking one seal: waddle it two steps, then shove a rival off its perch, brace against the next shove, or hold. Swept seals swim off, disgruntled. Last pod still dry owns the skerry.

How the skerry works

The rules of the rock

  • The skerry is an 11×9 grid. Every cell has a height from 0 to 6, generated from the match seed — both clients build the identical rock. The peak is always exactly three connected cells, so there is no unassailable pinnacle: the endgame is a melee for the last three rocks.
  • The tide is public. A cell is sea when its height is at or below the water level. The forecast bar at the top shows the whole schedule: 0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 — it holds for one round, then rises one level after every round, and by the end it covers everything. Cells that drown at the next rise shimmer on the board.
  • A round is one activation per living seal, the pods alternating. If a pod has no unwoken seals left, the other pod finishes its own in sequence. When every living seal has gone, the round ends and the tide steps.
  • An activation: waddle up to two orthogonal steps through dry, empty cells — climbing at most one level per step, descending freely, never voluntarily into the sea — then take one action, or none.
  • Shove a rival on an adjacent cell, directly away from you. Legal only if you stand no more than one level below them. You push them one cell, plus one more per full level you stand above them, capped at three. Anything shoved into the sea or off the rock is swept — it swims away and is out.
  • Chains: if the cell behind your target is occupied, the whole line shoves together — including your own seals. A body cannot be shoved up more than one level. If the line is blocked before it has moved at all, nothing moves: a solid bump that still spends your action. If it is blocked part-way, it simply stops there.
  • Brace instead, and the seal digs in until its next activation: it counts one level taller when someone shoves it (so a rival one level below cannot shove it at all, and any shove reaches one cell less), and no push can move it more than one cell.
  • Hold is always legal. Nothing can stall the match — the tide ends it.
  • The tide sweeps every seal standing on a newly drowned cell, both pods at the same instant.
  • Winning: the moment a pod has no seals left on the rock, the other pod owns the skerry. If both pods go under in the same instant — one tide step, or one chain that clears both ways — it goes to the pod whose last seal stood higher; if level, to the pod with more activations left unspent that round; if still level, to the east pod.
  • Turn order is a fixed public schedule: the pod that opens round one, two, three… runs west, west, east, west, east, west, east. The pods alternate from round two on; the west pod opens both of the first two because the east pod gets the last word in round one — and because in round one the shores are too far apart for either pod to touch the other. Twelve hundred games of self-play settled it: the seats come out 50.2 / 49.8.
Nothing is hidden. Both clients hold the same state, build the same rock from the same seed and compute the same result — there is no dice and no fog. Every shove you can see is previewed by the same code that resolves it, chain and splash included.
