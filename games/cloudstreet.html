CLOUDSTREET

Late afternoon, high summer. You have no engine — only a white sailplane, a long golden valley running east, and about seven hours of usable air. The rising columns that keep you up are invisible. You find them the way real pilots do: by ear, on the variometer, and by reading the ground. Dark plowed fields and warm village roofs make lift. Lakes and shaded forest eat it. Reach the airfield before the evening air goes glassy and still.

1Beeps mean lift. Faster and higher → stronger climb. When it sings, circle in it. A low moan means you are falling out of the sky.
2Clouds and hawks mark thermals. The cumulus drifts downwind of its core — aim a little upwind (right) of the cloud. Hawks circle the low ones.
3Slow down in lift, push through sink. And when the clouds line up into a street, fly straight underneath it — you climb without ever stopping to circle.
