CUTOFFSKELWORTH BRIDGE  →  RAVENSGARTH  ·  THE 5.38 STOPPER  ·  1954

You are driver and fireman on a Black Five: 345 tons on the drawbar, three timetabled legs over the Wrayfell bank. The boiler is a bank account — the fire lends you steam, the regulator spends it, and the timetable does not care. Fire little and often, notch the cutoff back once she is rolling, mind the wet railhead on the bank, and fill the glass before the summit.

Enginemanship

Steam is made by the fire, and the fire is drawn by the exhaust — work her harder and she burns harder, but the boiler can only make so much. Watch MADE / USED on the backhead: draw more than you make and the needle falls. Notch back — full regulator on a short cutoff does the same work on less steam.

Keep the fire meter in the green band: a thin fire makes no steam, a thick one shuts the air out, and a shovelful every dozen seconds holds it. Build her up before the bank, not on it. Water reads high on a climb and falls away over the summit, so fill the glass before the top. The vacuum brake takes three or four seconds to bite and seven to let go — and the sand is only good for half a minute a leg, so save it for the wet.
