KILO

TWO PASSES · ONE MEASURED KILOMETRE · DAWN

A jet hydroplane on a glass-calm mountain lake. A water speed record is not one run — it is two passes through the same measured kilometre, one in each direction, inside a single attempt window, and the mark is the average of the two. The standing outright record is 511.11 km/h, set in 1978. Get both passes done at any speed and you are on the board.

Two passes through a measured kilometre, one each way, inside the window. Your mark is the average. Standing record: 511.11 km/h.

The lake remembers. Your outbound pass leaves a real wake on the water that spreads and settles over two or three minutes. Come back down your own line too soon and that chop will hammer the sponsons of a boat already riding on centimetres. Run wide inside the gate, or wait — while the clock runs.

Full throttle to get over the hump at around 95 km/h. Above 350 the hull goes light and the nose wants to fly: trim nose-down or you will blow over near 440. Trim too far and you will spear in. Pop the chute to shut down before the basin.
