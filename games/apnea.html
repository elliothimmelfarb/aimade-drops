APNEA

An ama diver works a pearl bed on one breath. The sea holds you up at the top and pulls you down at the bottom — and every metre you fall for free must be bought back on the way home.

buoyancy vs depth  ·  up  →  the doorway  →  freefall
1
Lungs compress. Above 10 m the sea pushes you up. From 10–14 m you hang neutral — the doorway. Below it you sink for free, faster the deeper you go.
2
The dive reflex. Depth slows your heart from 70 to about 40 bpm, and a slow heart sips oxygen. Stillness is cheap. Kicking costs four times as much.
3
Shallow-water blackout. The last 10 m of the climb is where divers fail. Low on air there, your sight tunnels and your hands go slow.
THE BAG IS NOT YOURS YET: pearls only count once you touch the boat — black out and you lose everything you were still carrying. Air is free on top: swim out on the surface, then drop straight down.
