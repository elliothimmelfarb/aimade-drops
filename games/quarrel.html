Quarrel

a quarrel is a pane of glass · and a crossbow bolt

You and your rival share one commission. Take a quarry of glass from the bench and lead it into the window beside glass already set. Every unbroken run of your own colour is worth its size squared — four panes joined score sixteen, four panes scattered score four. You each carry two bolts. Loosing one shatters any pane but the boss, and the glass is gone from the world. Fill the lancet; the guild counts the light.

Articles of the commission

The full rules

The window

Fifty-one diamond quarries in a pointed lancet. One boss of clear grisaille is already leaded into the heart of it; the boss can never be shattered, and every other pane grows outward from glass already set.

Your turn — one of two things

Glaze. Take any one of the five quarries on the bench and lead it into any empty cell that touches a glazed pane along an edge. The bench refills from the bag.

Loose a quarrel. Spend one of your two bolts to shatter any single glazed pane except the boss. That glass is destroyed — it does not go back in the bag — and the cell is empty again. It costs your whole turn.

The count

Every edge-joined run of one colour scores its size squared. Your score is the sum over your three colours. Grisaille scores for nobody — it is a wall, and a wall in the right place is worth more than a pane of your own.

The end

The commission closes when the window is full, or when the bench is empty at the start of a turn. There are fifty-three quarries for fifty empty cells: if all four bolts fly between you, the window ends one light short and the guild will see the gap. Highest score wins; if the scores are level, the most panes of your own colours; if still level, the second glazier takes it. The tithe of one point to the first glazier is fixed compensation for the second's last word — it is shown in the score and counted honestly.
