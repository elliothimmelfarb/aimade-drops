SURFMAN

U.S. LIFE-SAVING SERVICE · 1884 · A NIGHT GALE · THREE WRECKS

Half past two in the morning and the surfman on the south patrol has burned his Coston light. A schooner has gone on the outer bar. You are the keeper. The beach cart is down, the sand anchor is buried, and the Lyle gun is laid on the wreck — you have five shots and a gale in your teeth.

The gun throws a shot that tows its line, and that line is heavy. It sags behind the projectile and drags it down: the further the shot flies, the more line it is pulling. Any shot that falls in the sea comes back soaked, and a wet line is a third heavier again. Powder and elevation are all you have.

Land the shot over the rigging and her people bend on the whip and the hawser. Then you haul them in, one at a time, in a breeches buoy. Mid-span the hawser hangs low and the sea comes through it. Haul hard in the troughs, ease at the crests — and a survivor caught under a genuine breaker is torn away. Meanwhile the seas are beating her to pieces. When the hull goes, whoever is still aboard goes with her.
