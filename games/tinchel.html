A ring of hunters on the dusk moor

Tinchel

One red hart stands in the middle of the moor. Nobody may move it — it answers only the ground. Each turn you set one stone (it fears them) or one of your three baits (it cannot resist them), then the hart takes two nervous steps of its own. Draw it home into your glen and the hunt is yours.

How the moor works

The rules of the tinchel

  • The board is a hexagon of 61 hexes. The lower five are Glen Doran; the upper five are Glen Cairnross. Your own glen is always drawn at the bottom of your screen.
  • The hart starts in the middle. You never move the hart. On your turn you make exactly one placement, then the hart takes two steps, one after the other.
  • Stones (unlimited): the hart will never step onto one, and it shies away from stones near it. Every stone frightens it — yours included.
  • Bait (three each, ever): the hart is drawn to it far more strongly than it is pushed by a stone. When it steps onto bait it eats it and the bait is gone for good — whoever set it. Bait can be stolen by tempo.
  • Left alone, the hart drifts back to the deep moor. To bring it to an edge you must build a real ring of stones, not one stone.
  • It will not step straight back onto the hex it just left unless it has no other choice, and it holds its line a little once it is running.
  • Cornered: if every neighbouring hex is stone or off the moor, the hart panics and leaps two hexes clear in the least-feared direction. The moor can never lock.
  • Nothing may be set in either glen. The glens are open ground for both estates.
  • The hart ending any step in a glen decides the match at once — including your own placement driving it into the other glen. The preview will tell you: green means you win, red means you lose.
  • Every stone is permanent, so the moor strictly fills and no match can run forever. In the freak case that no legal placement remains, the hart is judged to the nearer glen; dead level is a draw.
The hart's answer is fully deterministic. Both players' clients compute the identical result from the same match state and the same seed — there is no hidden information and no dice.
