HELIOTROPE

coupe de lumière · 2087 · earth–moon

You have the helm of a gossamer lightsail yacht in low Earth orbit. There is no engine, no fuel, no reaction mass — only eight hectares of aluminised film and the pressure of sunlight on it. Trim the sail, climb the spiral, thread four gates, and cross the finish ring at the Moon before MARIGOLD and SPINDRIFT do.

Sunlight always blows from the same quarter. You cannot tack into it — you can only lean, and let the orbit do the rest.

The dial reads cone° / trim° — the sail's angle to the sun, and its angle to your course. The dashed green line is the trim that climbs fastest right now. Sail load rises with F·cos²α — the flatter you hold the sail to the sun, the more thrust and the more strain. In a flare squall, feather it until the load bar sits just under the tear line, or furl and give up the gust.
