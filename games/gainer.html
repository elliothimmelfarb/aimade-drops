GAINER

SEA-CLIFF DIVING MEET · FIVE LEDGES · 8 TO 27 METRES

Sunset over a narrow inlet. Five ledges up one cliff and a rock apron at the bottom that the sea only just covers. The swell breathes. From the low ledges it is scenery. From 21 and 27 metres the channel is deep enough only near the top of a set — and you are two and a half seconds in the air, so you do not leap when it looks deep, you leap so that you arrive when it is. Watch the sets. Pick your moment.

In the air you are torque-free: whatever rotation you leave the ledge with is all you get. Tuck and you spin faster; stretch and you slow down. That is the whole craft — ride the tuck through the somersaults, open up at exactly the right instant to kill the spin, and go in vertical and tight. Leaping outward while rotating backward is the gainer, and the judges pay for it.

HOW A DIVE IS SCORED

Five judges give execution out of 10 — how vertical you are at entry, how straight and tight, and how small the splash. The high and low cards are thrown out and the middle three are added, then multiplied by difficulty: more somersaults, gainer direction and the height of the ledge. Rocks, a bottom strike in shallow water, or a flat smack all score zero — but the meet always runs all five rounds.
