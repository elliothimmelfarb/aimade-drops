HALFSPAN

DAWN MAIL RUN · 6.1 KM · 10 GATES

Your machine is a wing-in-ground-effect craft: below half a wingspan of altitude the sea gives the wing a cushion — extra lift, almost no induced drag. It is built for that cushion and cannot fly out of it. Climb and it mushes, bleeds speed and settles back. So you run the mail at three to five metres over a live swell, and every ship, stack and girder asks the same question: thread it low, or pop over and pay. Cross the finish line, then get her down and stopped before the seawall.

Below half a wingspan the sea gives your wing a cushion. Climb out of it and the craft mushes and bleeds speed. Run six kilometres at four metres, then land and stop before the seawall.
