IMPASTO

ACROBATIC DOUGH TOSSING · FIVE TICKETS · THREE DOUGHS

A competition stage under tungsten. Five order tickets come down the rail, each naming a diameter and a clock. You start every ticket with one proofed ball about seven and a half inches across, and there is exactly one thing in the world that will make it wider: spin. Centrifugal force pulls the rim outward, and once the dough is stretched past its elastic yield it does not come back — that permanent creep is the whole ratchet.

But the dough has a fixed amount of itself. Every inch of width is paid for in thickness, and thin dough is weak dough: springs soften, the tear limit falls, and the wide showpiece you have spent forty seconds building is one greedy toss from a rip. You have three doughs for the entire shift. A tear, a drop on the deck, or a kiss from a pendant lamp eats one.

HOW A PIE IS SCORED

Accuracy dominates and peaks at the exact ticket diameter — inside the ±1″ band is a good pie. Under the floor the peel refuses you (no dough lost); over the ceiling you may still serve, at a penalty. Evenness reads the spread of thickness across the field — over-tossing thins the middle. Style banks toss apex, clean-catch streak and your biggest single-toss gain. Speed pays for whatever clock is left. A timed-out or abandoned ticket scores nothing.
