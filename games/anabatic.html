ANABATIC

RIDGE RACING SAILPLANE · NO ENGINE · 6.8 KM OUT AND BACK

Late afternoon, ten metres a second of wind across the ridges, and no engine anywhere on this aircraft. The windward face throws the wind upward in a narrow band of lift — sit a wingspan or two off the trees and you climb at four metres a second; slide away from the face and it dies; cross to the lee and you get sink and rotor. Run the gates north up Ridge A, then cross the gap — a kilometre of ridge simply missing, with one band of rising air off a sunlit field for whoever gets it wrong. Round the pylon and come home. Speed and height are the same currency. The 120 kg of water in your wings buys penetration and costs climb — dump it and it is gone for good.

No engine. The windward face of the ridge is the only lift there is — fly close to it and climb, drift off and sink. Twelve gates north, across the gap, round the pylon, home. Water ballast buys speed and costs climb, and the dump is one-way.
