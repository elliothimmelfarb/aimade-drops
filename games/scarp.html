Isle of Scarp · Outer Hebrides · 28 July 1934

SCARP

Western Isles Rocket Post · Contract Trial

The sound is too rough for the mail boat and the GPO inspector is standing on the machair with his watch out. You have a launch rail, a keg of powder, ten hand-made casings and today's sack of 80 letters. Put 45 of them on the white sheet at Harris before the tide turns.

Every casing is different. Pack past its rated burst pressure and it lets go mid-burn — and the letters come down as burning confetti, exactly as they did for Herr Zucker.

Powder is rangePeak chamber pressure climbs steeply with the charge. The gauge predicts it; the casing's rating is stamped on the rack.
The wind aloft liesThe flag reads the beach. The clouds read the height the rocket actually flies through.
The chute is the aimBlow the nose too fast and the canopy tears. Too late and she spikes in. Deploy time is your fine adjustment.

How a shot works. Predicted peak pressure must stay under the casing's rating — the hatched band is hand-packing tolerance, so leave yourself a little. Airspeed above 50 m/s tears the canopy: watch the dynamic-pressure bar in flight and deploy when it falls into the green. Letters on the white sheet are delivered; letters that land safe but wide are carried back to the sack. The sea takes everything.
