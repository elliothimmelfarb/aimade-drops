PARISON

Stourbridge · 1893 · the night move

Inside the cone it is never quite dark. You are the gaffer: pipe across the rails of the chair, a gather of metal glowing on the end of it, a docket of commissions. A move is a run of work — and you are paid by the move, so the bell is what beats you.

Glass at this heat is a very slow liquid, and everything you do to it is permanent within seconds. Blow and the parison inflates, slower as the wall thins. Turn, and keep turning — stop and it droops off the pipe and falls. Reheat at the glory hole, because cold glass will not move and forcing it cold cracks it; overblow a thin hot wall and it bursts. Work the piece against the faint pattern behind it, then crack it off.

PAUSED — CONTROLS

Primary
W holdblow. Airflow ramps in smoothly; the bubble inflates more slowly as it grows and the wall thins. Thin + hot + hard blow = burst.
S holdreheat. The piece swings into the glory hole; the far end takes the most heat. Hot glass flows, cold glass will not move.
A D holdturn the pipe. Gravity is always pulling one side down — only continuous turning spreads it evenly. Stop with a hot piece and it droops off-axis and drops.

How a piece is paid

Match compares the top and the bottom of your piece against the pattern along the whole axis — so a sagged piece is punished twice, once for shape and once for being off-centre. The points curve is gentle below 85% and steep above it. Speed pays for whatever is left of the piece’s par time, and heat-craft pays for the time you kept the glass in its working band instead of scorching or freezing it. Below the acceptance floor of 50% the piece is a reject and pays almost nothing. A burst, a drop or a crack costs you the seconds it takes the assistant to bring a fresh gather — that is the whole penalty, and it is enough.
